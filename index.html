About me

I'm a machine learning research scientist based in Montréal, with a PhD in robust and multimodal learning and industry experience applying it to video understanding. What I enjoy most is designing new methods, drawn from the literature or built from scratch, to solve real problems, especially around how models represent images, video, audio and text.

Most recently, at Coactive AI, I finetuned LLMs with reinforcement learning (GRPO) to write prompts for video tagging, outperforming GPT-5.2 and replacing a paid API. I also calibrated embeddings across four modalities and designed methods to fuse vision, text and audio. Earlier, during my PhD, I worked on industrial anomaly detection with Matrox Imaging (now Zebra Technologies) through a research chair.

My PhD at ÉTS Montréal (LIVIA), advised by Christian Desrosiers and Ismail Ben Ayed, focused on test-time adaptation: letting models adapt to new domains at inference time, without labels. My papers were among the early work in this area, including some of the first methods for adapting vision-language models such as CLIP, and were published at NeurIPS, CVPR, ICCV and WACV. I also did a research stay at the Institut des Systèmes Intelligents et de Robotique (ISIR), Sorbonne Université, with Nicolas Thome.

These days I'm reading about self-supervised representation learning, world models and energy-based models. I also enjoy teaching and mentoring. I'm open to research scientist and research engineer roles, so feel free to reach out.
